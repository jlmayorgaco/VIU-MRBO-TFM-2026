\begin{viuabstract}{Abstract}
This thesis studies cooperative transport of heterogeneous payloads through
three subproblems: SP1 forms coalitions and assigns roles; SP2 approaches,
docks, transports, and replaces members; SP3 coordinates routes and reservations. The primary \emph{Cargo} mode models a supported
payload as a planar rigid body.

Three reviews with declared protocols ground the problem: structured
(3\,014 records, 59 canonical works), industrial (fourteen precedents), and
patent (167 records). The proposal combines LP/MILP references, potential games, primal--dual
dynamics, integer closure, and explicit contracts between layers. Formal results cover quota regulation, local Cargo
stability under fixed contact and exact wrench, termination of
route improvements, and the impossibility of guaranteeing global optimality when
disconnection hides indistinguishable instances with incompatible optima.
Experiments use paired worlds, explicit seeds, and oracles declared as ceilings.

SP1 regulates quotas, separates heterogeneous capacity, and removes nominal
false feasibility at the SP1--SP2 interface in two of six geometries,
identically across seeds. SP2 exhibits an adverse safety--progress trade-off and
restores all recoverable certificates in the static benchmark; the worst
equilibrium of its repair game has no uniform bound. In SP3, reservation reduces
deadlocks in the discrete catalogue; the congested pilot delivers nothing with
any method, leaving that regime uncharacterised, not diagnosed.
Cargo completes almost all missions without chaining all prior mechanisms. An
integration architecture certifies, per block, exact potential, geometric
separation, and an execution budget; its replay in CoppeliaSim was admitted
under a criterion requiring the expected outcome, so it documents reproduction,
not confirmation. The temporal contrast of H3 was not supported.

The contribution is a verifiable architecture of contracts and local games with
a planar functional composition. It does not establish global hybrid stability,
social optimality, fully distributed coordination, or industrial validity.
Instrumented contact, hardware, and caging remain outside the reported evidence.

\end{viuabstract}
