\noindent{\sffamily\bfseries\color{VIUOrange}\fontsize{18}{22}\selectfont Abstract}\par
\vspace{0.8em}

This Master's Thesis studies coalitions of autonomous mobile robots for transporting heterogeneous loads. An assignment may meet the requested robot count and still fail because of limited capacity, poor contact geometry, insufficient dynamic authority, collision, or stale information. A \emph{physical coalition} passes these checks without conflating static acceptance with transport success.

The main contribution is A0--FULL, a cumulative chain evaluated on one Euler--Lagrange plant. Its stages add local preference, integer quorum closure, effective capacity, contact complementarity, dynamic reserve with HOCBF, and recovery under network degradation. SP1--SP8 provide modular evidence rather than one end-to-end execution.

The protocol was frozen before opening the confirmatory seeds. It comprised 2,160 paired runs after precision-only extensions; inference used McNemar tests, paired bootstrap intervals, and Holm correction.

Under scarcity, integer closure increased success by $0.67$, effective capacity added $0.33$, and wrench closure then reduced success by $0.18$ after removing traction reserve. Paired contact search added $0.60$ under torque; the last two stages added $0.24$ and $0.62$ under an obstacle and dropout. FULL achieved $0.86$--$1.00$ success but retained $14\%$ failures in the most adverse regime. Its selector uses global candidates: this is a recovery proxy, not evidence of fully distributed local closure.

The theoretical development establishes a practical ISS result under a common Lyapunov matrix and explicit assumptions. The scope is planar simulation; the thesis makes no claim of physical validation, demonstrated functional safety, or universal superiority.
