\begin{viuabstract}{Abstract}
This master's thesis designs and evaluates an interpretable hybrid architecture
for forming coalitions of heterogeneous mobile robots and transporting one
payload in the Cargo mode. The approach combines potential games, integer
closure, service thresholds, a planar wrench guard, pose control, safety
filtering, failure repair, discrete route reservation, and versioned neighbour
messaging. It is not presented as fully distributed: several closures,
aggregates, per-payload optimisers, and the simulator's attribute registry retain
explicit global dependencies.

The study decomposes the problem into SP0--SP8 and combines proofs, paired
worlds, size-limited oracles, baselines, ablations, and 95\% confidence
intervals. The formal results characterise assignment and quota equilibria, the
integrability of heterogeneous service, the KKT--equilibrium correspondence of
the wrench game, local stability of pose control under fixed contacts, and
finite termination of the repair and route games.

Across 600 worlds, the mechanical guard reduced false positives by 0.333. The
repair game restored the additive certificate in all 360 recoverable cases. The
integrated Cargo campaign combined recruitment, dynamic-unicycle docking,
reduced planar transport, an obstacle, one failure, and replacement; it
completed 359 of 360 missions without a payload--obstacle intersection during
transport. The predicted time advantage of the spatial cost was not supported.
In SP8, 900 worlds with up to 128 robots showed that periodic retransmission
increased the conflict-free rate by 0.054 at the cost of 261.3 additional
messages per agent, while quality collapsed at the largest nominal size. A
kinematic CoppeliaSim pilot checked script geometry and exposed deadlocks; it did
not validate physical dynamics or industrial performance.

The architecture therefore operates as a planar composition within the tested
regimes. A global hybrid guarantee, three-dimensional support, wheel--ground
contact during transport, switching networks, hardware, and pushing through
caging remain outside the validated scope.

\keywords{Keywords}{multi-robot coordination, autonomous mobile robots, coalition formation, potential games, cooperative transport}
\end{viuabstract}
