\begin{viuabstract}{Abstract}
An explainable distributed architecture coordinates heterogeneous robot
coalitions and the transport of a planar payload. It combines potential games
and integer closure with certificates for capacity, wrench, pose, safety,
recovery, and communication.

The formal results characterise equilibria for homogeneous assignment and
variable coalition sizes, prove the degeneracy of linear deficit under
scarcity, and establish an integrability condition for heterogeneous services.
The marginal wrench field admits a potential and a
KKT--variational-equilibrium correspondence. The pose controller is locally
stable under fixed contacts and exact wrench realisation. Above a penalty
threshold, the recovery game's Nash equilibria equal the feasible
inclusion-minimal repairs; its price of stability is one, whereas its price of
anarchy has no uniform bound.

Evaluation used paired worlds. The mechanical guard reduced false positives by
0.333 across 600 worlds, and the repair game restored the certificate in all
360 recoverable cases. An integrated campaign combined restricted visibility, dynamic-unicycle
docking, reduced planar transport, an obstacle, a robot failure, and
replacement. The complete method finished 359 of 360 missions without a
payload--obstacle intersection during transport. Jointly removing the
certificate, waypoint detour, and safety filter reduced success by 0.996 in
obstacle regimes; disabling repair reduced it by 0.989 after failure. Under
degraded communication, the local and perfect-information variants completed
90/90 missions; the former used 328 messages per instance.

The mechanisms operated together for one planar payload and one failure; this
does not establish a global guarantee for the hybrid system. Messages query a global attribute registry,
and robot poses are reconstructed from the payload after docking. The predicted
time advantage of spatial cost was inconclusive. Three-dimensional dynamics,
switching networks, hardware, and pushing by caging remain outside the scope.

\keywords{Keywords}{distributed coordination, autonomous mobile robots, coalition formation, potential games, cooperative transport}
\end{viuabstract}
