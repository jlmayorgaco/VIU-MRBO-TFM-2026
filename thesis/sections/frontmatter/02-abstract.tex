\begin{viuabstract}{Abstract}
Coalition transport with heterogeneous mobile robots requires deciding which
robots can carry a payload together and preserving that assignment as the team
moves. This thesis evaluates a layered, interpretable architecture for this
task. Its primary \emph{Cargo} mode models a payload supported by a rigid
coalition and links potential games to integer closure, mechanical checks, pose
control, safety, repair, and neighbour communication. The implementation is
hybrid because several aggregates, per-payload optimisers, and the simulator
registry query global information.

A sequence of models extends one-to-one assignment to coordination over
imperfect networks. The formal analysis characterises assignment and quota
equilibria, establishes conditions for planar wrench feasibility, proves local
stability with fixed contacts, and proves termination of the finite repair and
route games. Methods are evaluated on paired worlds with bounded global
references, ablations, and 95\% confidence intervals.

In 600 worlds, the planar mechanical-feasibility check reduced the false-positive
rate by 0.333. The repair game restored all 360 recoverable certificates. The
Cargo demonstrator completed 359 of 360 missions without payload--obstacle
intersection during transport. The estimated timing effect did not support the predicted benefit of the
spatial-cost term. In SP8, retransmission increased the
conflict-free rate by 0.054 and added 261.3 messages per agent; the rate reached
zero at 128 robots. A three-dimensional kinematic pilot recorded persistent
deadlocks in all three congested scenarios.

The formal guarantees apply under their stated assumptions. The Cargo campaign
provides evidence of functional compatibility. Because the
integrated campaign substitutes several SP2--SP6 mechanisms, it does not
establish a system-wide distributed guarantee. Direct validation is still
needed for three-dimensional dynamics, wheel--ground contact, switching
networks, hardware, and caging-based transport.

  autonomous mobile robots, coalition formation,

\end{viuabstract}
