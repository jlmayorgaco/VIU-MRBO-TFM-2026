## Scope used by the old review

The review distinguished:

- coalition decision,
- physical feasibility,
- execution,
- recovery.

It coded “distributed” through decision authority, information, leader/server, and closure—not simply through local low-level control. Search/snowballing covered MRTA/coalition formation, shared-load transport, wrench/contact, imperfect networks, and recovery.

## Three mandatory readings from the old review

### Atomicity
Population games/GNE can produce continuous mass. Integer closure must be evaluated as part of the method rather than hidden as a post-process.

### From capacity to wrench
For Cargo, scalar capacity is insufficient. Physical feasibility requires the demanded wrench to belong to the coalition’s feasible wrench set under geometry, friction, and actuator limits.

### Scale
Separate simulated fleet size, hardware fleet size, robots per coalition, and concurrent payloads/tasks. Large simulated `N` is not equivalent to large physical deployment.

## Physical modality separation

1. Supported cargo — supported payload, wrench and actuator limits.
2. Rigid attachment — rigid formation / force distribution.
3. Pushing or caging — unilateral contact / non-prehensile geometry.
4. Grasping or arms — grasp matrix / force closure.

## Adversarial novelty notes from the old review

The old review explicitly warned that replacement is not novel by itself. It highlighted examples where replacement/reconfiguration already appears but with residual global/master scheduling or without local substitute selection from a larger heterogeneous fleet.

Its candidate gap was therefore conjunctive: the review had not identified one architecture combining local recruitment of a heterogeneous variable-size coalition from a larger fleet, physical certification of shared-load feasibility, online member replacement during transport, and no permanent global authority across coalition decision/execution.

**This bundle treats that gap as a hypothesis to falsify.**
